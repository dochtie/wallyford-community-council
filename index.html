Our mining village

Built on coal, proud of it

Wallyford grew up around its collieries and brickworks. The Miners’ Stone in St Clement’s Gardens names the men who lost their lives in the local pits. The community council commissioned it in 2009 at the request of miners’ families, and it was unveiled in 2010. Off Salters Road, the Wallyford Pug marks where the steam engine carried coal and bricks across the old level crossing.

We’re now working to restore the carved heritage stones around the bing.

Road markings on Masons Way

The double yellow lines and road markings along Masons Way have faded badly. That makes parking rules hard to enforce and crossings less safe near the Learning Campus. We’re asking East Lothian Council’s roads team to repaint them.

Planning

Restoring the heritage mining stones

The carved stones around the old Wallyford bing were made by sculptor Shaun Williamson in 1998, when the Edinburgh Green Belt Trust turned the spoil heap into green space. They tell the story of the village’s mining past, but time and weather have worn them down. We want to clean and restore them, and make the trail easier to find.

How you can help

  • Join a clean-up day once dates are set.
  • Share old photos or family stories of Wallyford’s pits. We’d love to put them on the trail.
  • Know of funding or a heritage group that could help? Get in touch.

Let’s get it fixed

Most problems in the village are fixed by East Lothian Council, but knowing who to tell can be confusing. Report it yourself in a few minutes, or let us do it for you.

Danger to life or a crime in progress? Call 999.

Don’t wait to report emergencies online. Other urgent numbers:

Report it yourself

Pick the problem to go straight to the right East Lothian Council form. You can report as a guest or sign in to a myeastlothian account to track progress.

  • Potholes and road faultsRoads, pavements, faded markings
  • Street lightsLights out, flickering, damaged

  • Dog fouling and dogsFouling, strays, dangerous dogs

  • TreesFallen, overhanging, dangerous
  • Winter and floodingGritting, grit bins, blocked drains
  • Something elseEast Lothian Council customer services

Five steps to a report that gets fixed

  1. Pin down the exact spot. “Outside 12 Masons Way, by the lamp post” beats “Masons Way”. Use the map on the form if there is one.
  2. Take a photo if it’s safe to. It shows the size of the problem and helps crews find it.
  3. Say why it matters. Mention if it’s near the school, a bus stop or a crossing, or if someone could get hurt.
  4. Keep the reference number you’re given, and the date you reported it.
  5. Nothing happened? Send us the reference number and we’ll chase it up with the council.

Planning applications

        
If a neighbour notification lands on your doormat, you normally have 21 days to comment.

        
    
          
  • Look up the application by its reference on the council’s planning portal.
    • 
          
  • Stick to planning issues: traffic, overlooking, noise, design, flooding. Loss of a view or property values don’t count.
    • 
          
  • Tell us too. The community council is a statutory consultee and can comment on applications in Wallyford.

What does a community council do?

        
Community councils are the most local level of representation in Scotland. We’re volunteers, independent of East Lothian Council. We pass on residents’ views, comment on planning applications, and organise things for the village like the Christmas lights. Anyone living in Wallyford can come to our meetings.
