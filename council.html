Wallyford Community Council

The Council

We're local people who give our time to make sure Wallyford's views are heard. We're independent of East Lothian Council, and our meetings are open to everyone.

What we do

Community councils are voluntary groups, independent of the local authority, that act as a voice for their area. We pass on the views and concerns of local people on everything from planning applications to potholes, and we're represented on the Musselburgh Area Partnership, which helps set local priorities and decide how money is spent on them.

In Wallyford, that means we:

  • respond to planning applications and licensing matters that affect the village
  • represent local views at the Community and Police Partnership meetings with Police Scotland, councillors and council officers
  • take part in the Musselburgh Area Partnership
  • pass on information to the community, such as temporary road closures (TTROs) and East Lothian Council consultations
  • push for things the village needs, like new pedestrian crossings
  • point people towards local services that can help
  • organise the Christmas Lights and the Torchlight Parade

We're a member of the Association of East Lothian Community Councils (AELCC) and take part in its meetings.

What we can't do

We aren't part of East Lothian Council and can't fix problems directly, but we can make sure the right people hear about them and keep asking until something happens.

To get something fixed quickly, report it to the right organisation first, then let us know if it keeps happening.

Christmas Lights and Torchlight Parade

First Friday in December

The Torchlight Parade is the biggest night in our calendar. On the first Friday of December, the parade sets off at 6.30pm from the Wallyford Miners' Charitable Society Club car park and, led by the Prestonlodge Pipe Band, makes its way up Salters Road to the Miners' Stone.

There we hold a short Christmas service with carol singing and lay a floral tribute to remember the 38 miners lost in Wallyford's pits. Then the Gala King and Queen switch on the village Christmas lights for the festive season.

The lights and the parade are paid for by our fundraising throughout the year.

How we're funded

East Lothian Council gives community councils an administration grant and funding through the Local Priority Scheme, which we use to improve the local environment for the benefit of the village.

We also raise money every year to pay for the Christmas Lights and the Torchlight Parade.
