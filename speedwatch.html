Why Speedwatch?

Speeding is one of the concerns raised most often at Community and Police Partnership meetings, where we take local issues to Police Scotland. It affects quality of life right across East Lothian.

Road safety is a priority for Police Scotland. Officers focus their speed enforcement on the roads where it will do most to prevent injuries, so quieter village streets don't always get regular checks. The police still respond to community concerns, and Community Speedwatch lets local people keep an eye on the streets that matter to us, in support of the police.

How it works

1

We check speeds

Trained, vetted volunteers use a speed detection device at agreed Speedwatch sites.

2

We pass on the details

Details of vehicles going over the limit go to Police Scotland, who carry out the appropriate checks.

3

The police follow up

Where needed, Police Scotland send the driver a follow-up letter.

Become a volunteer

Anyone who cares about road safety in the village can apply, and full training is given. Here's what happens after you get in touch.

  1. Vetting The Speedwatch Coordinator will give you a Police Scotland vetting form (Non-Police Personnel Vetting, Level 1, Parts 1 and 2) and send it to the Police Scotland Vetting Unit for you.
  2. Training Once your vetting is complete, the coordinator arranges your training. It covers:
    • road safety and health and safety awareness
    • personal safety advice
    • how to use the speed detection device
    • a practical demonstration at a Speedwatch site
  3. Code of conduct Every volunteer signs the Community Speedwatch code of conduct before taking part in any checks.
  4. Join a session Because the four areas share volunteers, you could help at checks in neighbouring villages as well as in Wallyford.

Police Scotland make the final decision on who can join a Community Speedwatch scheme.
